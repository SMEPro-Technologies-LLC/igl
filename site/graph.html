What a graph is

Identity as a served, digested artifact

Company graphs are boundaries

The actors of an organization, the authority each directly holds, the delegations between them, and the keys bound to each. Published as one canonical document with a unique id and a content digest, versioned on every edit.

Individual graphs are footprints

One identity's scope, grants, and limits. Authority resolves under the MIN law: structure can only narrow it, and only an explicit delegation can reach a higher scope, clamped and audited.

Receipts are double-bound

Every governed turn seals a signed receipt carrying two digests: the constraint matrix that governed the content and the identity graph that governed the actor. A third party proves both from the artifact alone.

How it works

The claim that survives an audit

A model does not understand a graph id, and GRAPH does not pretend it does. What happens is stronger, because it is provable: the runtime resolves the graph before the model computes, fails closed if the document does not reproduce its digest, refuses before a single model call if the actor does not exist in the graph, and fuses the resulting constraint into the model's own arithmetic.

01 PROVISION

Describe or build

Provision the graph in the studio: actors, authority, delegations, bound keys. Every publish is a new version.

02 DIGEST

Seal the version

The document gets a content digest. Raise an authority by one decimal and the digest no longer reproduces.

03 RESOLVE

Govern the turn

Any IGL surface resolves the graph by id, verifies it, and resolves the acting identity under the MIN law before the model runs.

04 RECEIPT

Prove it later

The signed receipt binds the graph digest and the matrix digest. Anyone holding it can verify which versions governed the turn.
